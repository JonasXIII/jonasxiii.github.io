Method & caveats

How the expected value is computed (and why it's exact)

A pack is one of a handful of variants, each a fixed set of slots. Every slot is one draw from a named sheet, a weighted list of cards. Within a sheet, the chance of a given card is its weight divided by the sheet's total weight; every sheet's weights sum exactly to its total, so each sheet is a proper probability distribution.

By linearity of expectation, the expected value of the whole pack is just the sum of the expected value of each slot; this holds regardless of duplicates or any dependence between slots, so no simulation is needed. Collapsing the variants gives an expected number of draws from each sheet per pack:

The value of one draw from a sheet is Σ (weight ÷ total) × price over its cards, using the foil price for foil sheets and the non-foil price otherwise. Multiply each sheet's per-draw value by its expected draws per pack, sum, and that's the pack EV.

Market price, not sell price. Prices are Scryfall's market/median (roughly TCGplayer). What a shop or buylist actually pays you is materially lower, and singles have fees and shipping. Treat EV as gross retail value, not cash-in-hand.
Missing prices count as $0. Any card without a listed price in the chosen currency is skipped, so the EV is a slight under-estimate when data is missing, and $0 for a pack that hasn't released yet. The status line reports how many cards were affected.
Prices are volatile and per-condition. These are near-mint market snapshots; they drift daily and don't reflect condition or regional supply.
Pack cost is whatever you enter. There's no reliable, CORS-friendly live feed for sealed-product retail prices, so "your cost per pack" is a manual field, not a fetched one.
